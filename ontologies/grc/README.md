# grc: controls and audit evidence

A vocabulary for compliance work: controls, the evidence that satisfies
them, the assessments that test them, and the findings and exceptions
that fall out. It exists to back a specific claim in the spec (§5.5):
that "our process requires review" can be verified cryptographically
instead of taken on trust.

## The auditor verifies instead of trusting

A traditional audit collects screenshots into a shared folder and asks
the auditee to promise they are real. Here, each piece of evidence is a
document identified by the hash of its bytes, collected by a registered
service identity whose metadata names the collector version — and the
collection pipeline itself runs in trusted hardware, so its identity is
provable. The auditor holds a grant scoped to the relevant framework's
data, syncs that slice of the graph, and verifies everything from the
log and public keys alone. No trust in the auditee's word is required.

## Exceptions are recorded, not hidden

Every real compliance program has exceptions, and most tools hide them.
Here an `Exception` is a first-class record with a required
justification and expiry date. Creating one requires the owner's signed
approval plus a cooling-off period, and the approval record is
permanent. The audit question "what did you waive, when, and who
signed off" is a query over the log.
