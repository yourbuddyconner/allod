# research: claims and evidence

This package defines a vocabulary for research graphs where every claim
can be checked. Each claim traces to the exact bytes of its sources, the
record of how it was extracted travels with it, and the question "show
me every document this claim rests on" has a mechanical, verifiable
answer (spec §5.1).

## Contents

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| [ontology.yaml](ontology.yaml) | Questions, claims, sources, quotations, datasets |
| [taxonomy.yaml](taxonomy.yaml) | Review stages: unverified, verified, published |
| [policy.yaml](policy.yaml) | Review requirements for each stage, and rules for machine-extracted claims |
| [examples/extraction.yaml](examples/extraction.yaml) | A claim extracted by a model, its evidence chain, and its promotion to verified |

## Two rules do most of the work

**Claims cannot exist without evidence.** The validation rule
`claims-cite-evidence` requires every new `Claim` to arrive in the same
changeset as at least one `cites` link to evidence. An unsupported
assertion fails admission structurally, before any reviewer looks at it.

**How a claim was produced matters.** A claim asserted by a person, one
derived by a parser, and one extracted by a language model each carry
different origin metadata (spec §8.2), and the policy treats them
differently. A model-extracted claim must arrive with proof of which
pipeline produced it — so it is exactly as trustworthy as that measured
pipeline, and a reader can check which pipeline it was.

## The review stages

Publishing builds on verification: the `review/published` classification
inherits every requirement of `review/verified` and adds the owner's
sign-off (spec §4.1). Moving a claim up a stage is a classification
change, which is a governed change, which produces a signed approval
record. The full editorial history of every claim — who verified it,
against which version of the policy — is in the log.

## Why this matters now

Machine-generated text is cheap, so the value has moved to knowing where
a claim came from. A research graph in this shape gives a reader a
choice the open web cannot: accept a claim, or walk its chain down to
the quotation, the source bytes, and the identity of whatever extracted
it.
